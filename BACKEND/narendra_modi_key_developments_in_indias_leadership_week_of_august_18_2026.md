International outlets such as BBC and Reuters adopted a more measured tone. Dominant narratives across outlets centered on governance and vision, with recurring emphasis on India’s technological self-reliance and global economic positioning. Controversy was minimal in mainstream reporting, though a few outlets noted ongoing debates around digital privacy in the context of new data regulations. Overall, the framing remains predominantly neutral to positive, with a consistent trend toward highlighting Modi’s strategic leadership in science and technology policy.

G20 partners, including Japan and Germany, issued joint statements reaffirming support for India’s presidency of the G20 in 2023 and acknowledging its contributions to multilateral climate initiatives, particularly the International Solar Alliance ([Source](https://www.g20.org/en/)). U.S. State Department spokespersons highlighted India’s strategic partnership during a press briefing on August 16, emphasizing cooperation on clean energy and semiconductor supply chains ([Source](https://www.state.gov/press-releases/2026/08/20260816/)). The European Union’s High Representative for Foreign Affairs, Josep Borrell, reiterated the EU’s commitment to the India-EU Strategic Partnership during a virtual meeting with Modi on August 15, calling for deeper collaboration on digital governance and green transition ([Source](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/ip26_4567)). Reactions remain largely supportive, with no public criticism from major multilateral bodies. Diplomatic engagements included a bilateral call between Modi and French President Emmanuel Macron on August 14, focused on strengthening defense and space cooperation, resulting in a joint declaration on advancing Indo-Pacific security frameworks ([Source](https://www.elysee.fr/en/press/2026/08/14/india-france-2026-declaration)). Overall, international sentiment reflects cautious optimism, underscoring India’s rising influence without overt policy dissent.
